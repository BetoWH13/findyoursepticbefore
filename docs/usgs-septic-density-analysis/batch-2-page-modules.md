## Module 4: Urban/Suburban Checklist

Purpose: Answer the user's exact concern: what should an urban-area homeowner do?

Checklist:

- Confirm whether the property has a municipal sewer account, not just a city address.
- Check closing documents, disclosures, old inspections, and property records.
- Search local health department or county septic records.
- Look for private well clues, large-lot patterns, old additions, sewer-edge streets, and lake/coastal neighborhood history.
- Call 811 before digging.
- Remember that 811 may not mark private septic tanks, septic lines, distribution boxes, or drain fields.
- Use septic-specific locating if records are missing and the project involves posts, trenches, grading, patios, pools, sheds, additions, or drainage work.

## Module 5: "A Sewer Line Nearby Does Not Prove Your House Is Connected"

Purpose: Make the urban angle concrete.

Content points:

- Some homes remain on septic after sewer expansion.
- Some neighborhoods are partially connected.
- Some properties have city mailing addresses but county or unincorporated utility arrangements.
- Some lots have abandoned, replaced, or poorly documented systems.
- The only safe answer is property-level verification.
